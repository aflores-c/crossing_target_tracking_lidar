## 2. Synthetic experiments (exact ground truth)

`docs/results/synthetic/synthetic_results.md` (generated by `synthetic_experiments`). Ray-cast
VLP-16 model (16 layers, 0.2°), flat ground, boxes. Matching gate 4 m, objects within 30 m.
**Position error includes the method's inherent bias**: the tracker estimates the centre of the
*visible* blob, ground truth is the box centre (up to ~half a vehicle size). Default configuration:

| scenario | HL ids per object | ID sw | false HL ids | precision | recall | pos RMSE [m] | vel RMSE [m/s] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 static scene | – | 0 | 0 | – | – | – | – |
| 2 car 10 m/s | car:1 | 0 | 3 | 0.79 | 1.00 | 1.17 | 1.04 |
| 3 turning car (ω −0.3) | car:3 | 4 | 3 | 0.79 | 0.73 | 1.74 | 1.75 |
| 4 car + motorcycle | car:3, moto:2 | 3 | 2 | 0.85 | 0.66 | 1.14 | 1.72 |
| 4b two pedestrians 0.8 m apart | 1, 1 | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | 0.93 | 0.21 | 0.08 |
| 5 occlusion behind parked van | cyclist:2 | 1 | 0 | 1.00 | 0.73 | 0.50 | 0.64 |
| 6 four road users | 1,1,2,2 | 2 | 6 | 0.92 | 0.82 | 1.14 | 1.37 |
| 7 parking car / new static box | 3, 1 | 2 | 2 | 0.86 | 0.21* | 0.99 | 1.56 |
| 8 moving sensor + odometry | car:1 | 0 | 3 | 0.61 | 1.00 | 1.06 | 1.15 |
| 9 slow 12 m bus (3 m/s) | bus:3 | 4 | 4 | 0.54 | 0.87 | 2.10 | 1.12 |

\* intended: once parked, objects are absorbed into the background (no more tracks at the end, asserted by the test).

Ablations (same table file):
* **Position-only clustering**: two passing pedestrians → ID switch and 5× velocity RMSE (0.43 vs 0.08 m/s);
  for vehicles it slightly reduces fragmentation (s4 car 2 ids instead of 3). The velocity criterion does
  what the paper claims (separates close objects with different velocities).
* **Shadow management off**: **no measurable difference** in any synthetic scenario with the defaults
  (W = 100, τ_bck = 0.4). Reason (derived and verified): one occluder hides a background cell for at most
  as long as it occupies its own lane cells, so with τ_bck < 0.5 the occluder is absorbed before the
  background is forgotten. The paper's failure case needs τ_bck > 0.5 or repeated occluders; the unit test
  `test_shadow_management_preserves_occluded_background` (τ = 0.6) shows the mechanism works.
* **Raw-count binarisation** (fixed 2-point threshold): on clean synthetic data comparable or slightly better;
  on the real bag it detects fewer moving tracks (§3).
* **No ego-motion compensation on moving data** is worse (test `test_8b`).

Weak points visible here: vehicles fragment into 2–3 high-level ids (new blob tracks start with v = 0 and
enter clustering with a lagging velocity); slow long vehicles are partly absorbed by the moving-average
background (s9: limited by τ_bck·W).

### Parameter selection
The paper gives no values. Defaults were chosen with small sweeps on scenarios 2–8 (commands in the
history of this work; summary): square closing 5, M_c/N_c = 5/7 (lets the velocity converge before
clustering: high-level ids/object 2.3 → 1.6), high-level R₂ = (1.0 m, 1.5 m/s), τ_d = 3 m, τ_v = 2 m/s,
W = 100 with τ_bck = 0.4 (static scene 0 false tracks, s9 recall 0.10 → 0.87 vs W = 50/τ = 0.25).
These are tuned on synthetic data and **not validated as optimal for the real crossing**.

## 3. Real bag `bag_go2_20261105_165413` (no ground truth — descriptive only)

Dataset facts in `docs/DATASET_REPORT.md`. Segments: stationary 40–137 s (odometry: 1.6° yaw / 2.5 cm
drift), walking 145–205 s. Outputs (CSV + plots) in `docs/results/bag_go2_20261105_165413/`
(`plots/*/trajectories.png`, `velocity.png`, `speed_accel.png`, `counts.png`, `latency.png`).

**Stationary segment (962 scans, all valid)**

| variant | blobs/frame | HL tracks/frame | HL tracks ≥ 20 frames | moving tracks (≥ 5 m) | near-static tracks (< 1 m; false-detection proxy) | restart proxy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default (odom registration) | 9.5 | 8.5 | 128 | 45 | 50 | 17 |
| shadow off | 10.0 | 9.1 | 134 | 44 | 49 | 16 |
| position-only clustering | 9.5 | 8.4 | 127 | 43 | 50 | 18 |
| raw-count binarisation | 8.9 | 7.6 | 106 | 36 | 42 | 13 |
| stationary assumption (no registration) | 12.1 | 10.8 | 159 | 41 | 65 | 21 |

Readings (proxies, not accuracy): odometry registration reduces spurious near-static tracks vs. the
stationary assumption (50 vs 65) — the standing Go2 sways/rotates slightly. Shadow management and the
clustering criterion make little difference on this segment. The raw-count baseline yields fewer moving
tracks (36 vs 45). "Near-static" tracks may also be real (standing pedestrians, waiting vehicles); without
annotations they cannot be separated from false detections. Qualitatively (`trajectories.png`), long
straight tracks at 3–7 m/s follow one line through the scene, and several tracks move at 1–1.4 m/s; this
is visual plausibility, **not** a validated accuracy claim.

**Walking segment (595 scans)**

| variant | valid frames | blobs/frame | HL tracks/frame | near-static proxy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| odom + per-point deskew (default) | 595 | 33.4 | 29.2 | 63 |
| CPD 3D only | 440 | 26.2 | 18.3 | 48 |
| CPD 2D (BEV) only | 595 | 55.9 | 51.0 | 86 |
| no compensation (negative control) | 595 | 59.2 | 57.6 | 71 |

* Ego-motion compensation is necessary (59 → 33 blobs/frame) but **insufficient while walking**: ~3.5× the
  stationary blob rate. Blob count grows with yaw rate; per-point deskew (added after this observation)
  reduced blobs at |ω| > 0.5 rad/s from 69 to 42 per frame. Perturbing the estimated odometry time offset
  by +50/+100/+150 ms increased false blobs (32 → 40/42/44), so the receive-time offset estimate
  (−2.730 s) is consistent. Remaining causes: newly visible areas and a background history that is
  corrupted during motion (W = 10 s). This matches the paper's operating assumption (the robot stands
  still before deciding); **tracking output while walking should not be trusted** without further work.
* CPD (paper's registration) alone: our implementation rejects 26 % of frames in 3D (quality gate) and is
  biased on synthetic data (1–2 m drift over a 4 m walk, §5). The fewer CPD-3D blobs partly reflect the
  rejected frames. Odometry stays the default (user decision).
* Per-frame CPD-vs-odometry disagreement (diagnostic run): median 1.1 cm / 0.04°, p90 7.6 cm / 0.52°.

**Full bag**: 2967 scans, 2964 valid (3 rejected odometry frames at start-up), 22.6 blobs/frame mean.

### Detection range (ROI 40 m vs 60 m)

"Vehicle-like" = high-level track with median speed ≥ 3 m/s, ≥ 10 m travelled, ≥ 20 observed frames
(speed proxy, no semantics, no ground truth). Runs `stationary_roi60` / `full_bag_roi60`
(`grid.roi_max_range=60, grid.size_x/y=120, input.max_range=80`).

| run | vehicle-like tracks | first detection p10/50/90 [m] | farthest range p10/50/90 [m] | max [m] | blobs/frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stationary, ROI 40 | 8 | 11 / 22 / 38 | 24 / 34 / 40 | 40.0 | 9.5 |
| stationary, ROI 60 | 12 | 15 / 31 / 57 | 25 / 50 / 59 | 59.4 | 12.6 |
| full bag, ROI 40 | 33 | 12 / 20 / 37 | 22 / 32 / 40 | 40.1 | 20.2 |
| full bag, ROI 60 | 45 | 14 / 23 / 55 | 22 / 41 / 59 | 59.7 | 30.0 |

* Tracks still end at the ROI boundary: the usable range on this bag is limited by configuration, not
  yet by the sensor.
* Beyond 45 m, 6 (stationary) / 13 (full bag) fast tracks are continuous with detections below 35 m
  (objects followed through the whole range). Another 21 / 172 tracks exist **only** beyond 45 m, most
  with < 2 m displacement (16 / 108) — likely clutter from 1–2 LiDAR layers (false-detection proxy).
* Latency (idle, stationary segment): 18.8 → 43.3 ms mean, p95 37 → 95 ms, i.e. at the 10 Hz limit.
* Default kept at 40 m. Velocity accuracy at long range is unverified (no ground truth).

## 4. ROS playback (Mode A)

Node fed by `ros2 bag play --clock 100 --start-offset 40` (only LiDAR + odometry): every output topic at
9.93 Hz (= input rate), pipeline 15 ms + publish 6 ms mean (earlier check), odometry clock offset
−2.739 s (offline: −2.733 s). Recorded 30 s of `/crossing_tracking/blobs` and compared with the offline
tool on the same interval (`compare_playback`): 322 common scans; 15 offline scans missing in playback
(subscriber drops near the end / start-up); equal blob counts 75 % (0–10 s), 97 % (10–20 s), 89 % (20–34 s).
The core is identical; differences come from the different first processed scan (background history
differs until W = 10 s of history is replaced), dropped scans, and sim-time `/clock` quantisation of the
odometry offset. RNGs are seeded per scan (stamp), so the same scan with the same history gives the same
result.

## 5. Registration (CPD) on synthetic data

Rigid CPD (own NumPy implementation of Myronenko & Song 2010, s = 1) on the s8 walk (0.8 m/s, 0.08 rad/s,
5 s): final error 1.1 m / 6.5° (3D, 144 ms/scan) and 1.7–1.9 m / 1–4° (2D BEV, 11–45 ms/scan); recovers a
known rigid motion exactly on a static point set (unit test). The drift is attributed to the scan-pattern
bias of scan-to-scan point matching with a 16-layer LiDAR (not verified further).

## 6. Performance (single process, idle machine, offline mode)

| segment | mean / p95 / max total [ms] | dominant stage (mean / p95) | effective rate | CPU | RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stationary (962 scans) | 18.8 / 37.0 / 64.0 | dynamic extraction 11.8 / 27.7 ms (shadow cones per blob) | 52 Hz | 1.06 core | 243 MB |
| walking (595 scans) | 52.2 / 87.4 / 111 | dynamic 34.9 / 58.2 ms, grid 8.8 / 24.4 ms (α recomputed when the sensor moves) | 19 Hz | 1.02 core | 247 MB |

Other stages: ground 3.0 ms, registration 0.2 ms (odom), low level 0.9–2.7 ms, clustering 0.3–0.8 ms,
high level 0.6–1.5 ms. The stationary case keeps up with 10 Hz with margin; the walking case is within
budget on average but its maximum (111 ms) exceeds one scan period. The shadow cone computation (a full-grid
pass per blob) is the identified bottleneck; restricting it to a bounding window is the next optimisation.
CPD in 3D costs ~140 ms/scan and cannot run at 10 Hz in Python. (Timings in the ablation tables of
`plots/*/comparison.md` were measured with 5 parallel jobs and are not representative.)

## 7. Not verified / pending

* Position/velocity RMSE, ID switches, precision/recall **on real data: pending annotations** (no ground
  truth exists for this bag). The paper's Fig. 17–18 comparison against manually selected LiDAR points
  could be reproduced once such annotations are made.
* Real-data false-detection rate: only proxies (near-static tracks), which mix true stationary road users.
* Behaviour on the Go2 onboard computer (only workstation timings).
* Live sensor operation (only bag replay).
* RViz rendering on screen.
* Optimality of every parameter; extrinsic calibration base_link → velodyne_go2 (hand-measured).
* Moving-sensor tracking quality on real data is poor (§3); CPD accuracy on real data is unknown beyond its
  disagreement with odometry.
